Sudhara Finance requires a web application with two separate access portals: an Admin Portal and a Customer Portal. The landing page should provide two clearly accessible buttons for Admin and Customer, and selecting either option should redirect the user to the respective login page. The application should maintain separate permissions and workflows for administrators and customers, while keeping customer registration, loan requests, loan records, installment tracking, payment updates, and notifications connected throughout the system.

The Customer Portal should provide both registration and login functionality. For new customers, the registration form should collect their full name, bank account number, WhatsApp number, email address, Aadhaar card, voter ID, PAN card, and a self photograph. The required documents and photograph should be uploaded through dedicated upload fields, with each uploaded file restricted to a maximum size of 150 KB. After successfully submitting the registration request, the customer should see a confirmation popup containing a message such as “Request received. You will receive your password and login credentials through email and WhatsApp within 24 hours.” The registration request should then become available in the Admin Portal for verification and approval.

Once a customer logs in successfully, the Customer Dashboard should display their account information and complete loan-related information. If the customer has never taken a loan, the dashboard should clearly display a message such as “No loan history found.” If the customer has one or more loans, each loan should be displayed separately with important information such as the loan amount, loan start date, current status, repayment duration, and a countdown timer showing the remaining loan period. Each loan should have an expand option that reveals additional details, particularly the installment schedule, including the installment amount, planned payment date, payment status, and the number of installments already completed.

Every pending installment should have a payment or contact-admin button associated with its scheduled date. When the customer selects this button, it should initiate a call or contact request with the administrator. The administrator should ask the customer for their unique customer ID to verify the account. Every customer must have a unique five-character-or-identifier-based customer ID beginning with “SH-”, followed by unique characters or numbers, for example “SH-XXXX”. After locating the customer using this ID, the administrator can confirm whether the customer wants to pay through cash or online payment. For an online payment, the administrator should be able to provide the required payment credentials or QR code to the customer. After the transaction has been successfully verified, the administrator can mark that particular installment as paid from the Admin Portal, after which the installment status should immediately be reflected as completed in the customer's dashboard as well as in the customer's payment history.

The installment system should automatically track scheduled payment dates. If an installment remains unpaid after its due date, a fine of ₹10 should be applied to that installment and reflected in the relevant loan record. The system should also generate notifications before each installment due date so that the customer is reminded in advance. Once the customer contacts the administrator and the administrator verifies the payment and marks the installment as completed, the installment date should be marked as paid and the customer should receive confirmation through email and, when WhatsApp integration is available, WhatsApp notification. After an installment is completed, the next scheduled installment should become active according to the predefined repayment schedule and should display its own payment/contact button.

The Customer Dashboard should also contain a “Request Loan” option. When the customer submits a new loan request, the system should collect the requested loan amount, the reason for the loan, and the preferred repayment duration. The duration should provide two loan-type options. The first type should contain four selectable radio-button durations of 6, 12, 18, and 24 months, while the second type should contain four selectable radio-button durations of 24, 48, 72, and 96 weeks. After submitting the loan request, a confirmation popup should inform the customer that the request has been received and that confirmation will be provided through email and WhatsApp. The customer should also have a button in the popup to return to the dashboard.

The system must prevent customers from submitting additional loan requests while a previous loan request is still being processed by the administrator. It must also prevent a customer from requesting another loan while they have an existing active loan that has not yet been completely cleared. Once the current loan has been fully repaid and closed, the customer should become eligible to submit another loan request according to the system rules. The status of a pending loan request should be visible to the customer so that they can understand whether it is awaiting approval, approved, rejected, or otherwise being processed.

Each approved loan should have a complete repayment timeline and countdown timer. The system should continuously calculate the remaining duration of the loan, and when the loan approaches its scheduled completion date, appropriate notifications should be sent to both the customer and the administrator. The notification should clearly communicate that the loan completion date is approaching. All loan timers, installment dates, payment statuses, completed installments, remaining installments, and applicable fines should remain synchronized between the Customer Portal and Admin Portal.

The Admin Portal should provide a dashboard containing several management sections for handling customer registrations, loan requests, customer records, loans, installments, and account management. One section should be dedicated to loan requests, where the administrator can view all submitted loan requests along with the relevant customer details, requested amount, reason, selected duration, and other necessary information. Each request should provide Approve and Reject actions. When a loan request is approved, the administrator must be able to configure the loan details, including the approved loan amount, repayment duration, installment schedule, and most importantly the installment amount that the customer must pay for each scheduled installment. The approved loan and its repayment information should then automatically become available in the customer's dashboard.

The Admin Portal should also contain a New Registration Requests section. This section should display all newly submitted customer registrations with their complete submitted information, uploaded documents, and photograph so that the administrator can review the application before approval. The administrator should have Approve and Reject actions for each registration request. After approving a customer registration, the administrator should be able to create the customer's login credentials. The system should generate or allow the administrator to create a unique customer ID beginning with “SH-” and followed by four unique characters or numbers, such as “SH-1234”, along with the required login credentials. These credentials should then be communicated securely to the customer through the configured notification channels.

The Admin Portal should provide a Customer Logs or Customer Management section where the administrator can search for customers by name or unique customer ID, as well as browse or scroll through the available customer records. Selecting a customer should open their complete profile and financial history, including registration information, current account status, all previous and active loans, loan amounts, loan dates, repayment durations, installment schedules, completed installments, pending installments, overdue installments, applicable fines, and overall repayment progress. Both the administrator and customer should be able to see how many installments have already been completed and how many remain outstanding, ensuring that the repayment information is consistent across both portals.

From the Customer Management section, the administrator should also have the ability to delete a customer account when required. Deleting a customer should trigger the appropriate account-related notifications to the relevant parties and should ensure that all associated customer data, loan records, and related properties are handled according to the application's data-management rules. The deletion process should preferably include a confirmation step to prevent accidental removal of customer records.

The Admin Portal should additionally include an Add Customer section that allows the administrator to manually create a customer without waiting for a public registration request. The Add Customer form should contain the same major customer information and document fields available in the normal customer registration process. After entering the customer's information, the administrator should be able to create the customer's account and generate the same type of unique “SH-XXXX” customer ID and login credentials. The newly created customer should then be able to access the Customer Portal using those credentials.

The application should maintain a complete and synchronized customer and loan data structure using MongoDB as the primary database. Customer profiles, registration requests, loan records, loan requests, installment schedules, payment statuses, fines, customer IDs, account statuses, and relevant activity logs should be stored in MongoDB with appropriate relationships between customers and their loans. Cloudinary should be used for storing customer photographs and uploaded documents instead of storing large binary files directly inside MongoDB. Nodemailer should be used for email-based registration confirmations, login credential notifications, loan-request updates, installment reminders, payment confirmations, overdue notifications, and other required system notifications. The architecture should also be prepared for future WhatsApp integration so that the same important notifications can later be delivered through WhatsApp without requiring major changes to the existing application structure.

The overall system should maintain a single consistent loan lifecycle between the Customer Portal and Admin Portal. A customer registration should move from submitted to approved or rejected by the administrator; an approved customer should receive unique credentials; a customer loan request should move through its administrative approval process; an approved loan should generate its installment schedule; each installment should move through pending, paid, or overdue states; overdue installments should automatically include the applicable fine; completed payments should immediately update the repayment history for both the customer and administrator; and once all installments are completed, the loan should be marked as cleared or closed. The system should ensure that every important status change is reflected consistently across both portals and that the appropriate email and future WhatsApp notifications are triggered automatically at each required stage.
